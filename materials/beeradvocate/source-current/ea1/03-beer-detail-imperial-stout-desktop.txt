Formerly known as Imperial Russian Stout

Ask any hardcore Stone enthusiast about our most legendary beers and you’re bound to hear mention of this one. Nearly jet black with a fluffy hot chocolate-colored head, a goblet of this obsidian wonder held to the sky could block out the sun. Redolent with dark chocolate and heavy roast up front, gracefully supported by nuances of coffee, black currant, molasses as it finishes… this beaut is ageable for years. Some of us are still enjoying our bottles from the early 2000’s. So, to look ahead, what will your stock of the 2022 edition of the Stone Imperial Stout be in 2037?

Copenhagen 9/12 2023. 35,5 cl can picked up at Meny, Borups Allé half a year ago. As "Stone Imperial Stout". Black and blue can with Stone's iconic Devil head.

Pours opaque dark brown with a big beige head. Stable. Settles as thin patch of foam unable of covering the surface of the beer. Moderate lacing.

Aroma is fairly intense with a dark malty and toasted odor hiding a sharp edge of dark alcohol Caramel, toffee and brown sugar. Dark chocolate and licorice. whiff of Calvados.

Light carbonation. Medium thick, oily, soft, slightly viscous, almost flat texture.

Flavor is intense with a strong sweetness followed and balanced by a strong bitterness. Aftertaste is bitter dark licorice. Lingering. Finish is rather dry.

Fine potent boozer.

Reviewed by nizzle8287 from Texas

5/5  rDev +15.5%
look: 5 | smell: 5 | taste: 5 | feel: 5 | overall: 5
Poured into a tulip pint at about 45F. Jet black and totally opaque with a desert sand colored and very fine head that lasts much longer than anticipated, leaving amazing streaks of lacing along the sides of the glass.
Smells of caramel and cocoa with a nice yeasty zest that isn't funky like it is in some other stouts.
Taste is sweet caramel up front with bakers cocoa in the middle and a nice dry finish. The flavors are similar to Oscar Blues' Ten-Fidy (the regular version).
Mouthfeel is amazing. The very fine head makes for a velvety mouthfeel similar to North Coast's Rasputin. The 10.5% ABV is covered up so well that I would have guessed this to be a 7-8% abv beer in a blind taste test.
Overall absolutely SUPERB job this year y'all! 100% will be buying this as long as the cold weather lasts, and/or as long as it remains on the shelf.

Poured into a chalice: color is solid black, head is tan with fine to small bubbles and presents one finger thick, head lingers with a solid top with frothy lacing on the glass sides.

Smell is boozy upfront, then roast, chocolate, toast, malt, earth and grassy hops.

Taste is more malt, roast, cocoa, light espresso, earth, grassed hops, boozy notes are more backing flavor; very good flavor and high glass imperial stout.

Mouthfeel is low/moderate carbonation, moderate sweetness, moderate bitterness; really easy on the boozy bite and flavor for such a big beer.

Overall a big beer that drinks lighter but brings forward the flavors you want in a big stout - chocolate, roast, light coffee, some booziness.

From a 12oz can dated "PKG" 10/24/24 into a stemless snifter.

Pours about the blackest I've seen a stout with about one and a half fingers of brown head. Great retention and lacing.

Some charred smoky and dark fruit aromas on the crack of the can. In the glass, bitter baker's chocolate is noticeable.

This is a bitter tasting stout. Baker's chocolate, dark chocolate, light coffee with a roasty flavor over it all.

I feel like this beer could be a little thicker but for what it is, the body works just fine. Low carbonation. Bitter aftertaste.

Great old-school imperial stout.
