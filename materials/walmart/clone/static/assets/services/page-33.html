FAQ

What is a benefits program?

A benefits program is assistance that helps eligible households pay for everyday needs like groceries and essentials. These programs are typically run by government agencies or community organizations and come with specific rules for who qualifies and what’s covered.

  • Eligibility varies: Factors can include income, household size, and location.
  • What’s covered differs: Some programs support food purchases, others provide general cash aid.
  • How you use it may vary: Accepted payment methods and eligible items can differ by store and region.

Walmart doesn’t determine eligibility or benefit amounts. It’s best to review details with the official program administrator and check which payment options appear for you at checkout.

Which assistance programs help with groceries and essentials?

There are federal, state, and local programs that may offer support for food and household necessities. The specifics—who qualifies, how much assistance is available, and what items are eligible—can differ widely by program and state.

  • Start with official sources: Visit your state or local government website to see available programs.
  • Confirm what’s covered: Some programs focus on food only; others offer broader cash support.
  • Check acceptance: At checkout, you may see which payment types are available for your order. Availability can vary by location and program.

For the most accurate information, verify details directly with the administering agency before you shop.

How do I shop here using my benefits?

You can often use eligible benefit payment methods when shopping, depending on your program and location. Steps can vary, but this general flow may help:

  1. Add eligible items to your cart (some programs limit what you can buy).
  2. Begin checkout and look for available payment options shown for your order.
  3. Add your benefit card/account if prompted and select it for the eligible portion.
  4. Choose pickup or delivery where available and review any applicable fees.
  5. Bring a backup payment method for items, fees, or amounts the benefit doesn’t cover.

Acceptance and item eligibility can differ by region and program, so the options you see may vary.
